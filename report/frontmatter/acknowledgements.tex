\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Acknowledgements}

Few people have never dreamed of financial freedom, of money that earns money while its owner
sleeps. I am no exception. The stock market is the most accessible place to chase that dream,
and for a data science student it is also an irresistible puzzle: thousands of new numbers
every day, and the promise that somewhere in them lies a pattern others have missed.

The Vietnamese market adds its own twist. In my experience, know-how here is closely guarded:
those who appear to have an edge rarely explain it, and what is shared in public is mostly tips
without method. Whether real alpha exists on the Ho Chi Minh City Stock Exchange or not, it is
not written down anywhere a newcomer can learn from. That alone made me want to look for myself.

There is, honestly, some ego involved as well: the wish to find out whether one student with
public data and careful methods can beat a market that many professionals do not. This thesis
is my chance to try that seriously, with costs, market rules and statistics strict enough that
the answer is believable whichever way it turns out. I also hope it is useful to those who come
after me: if it leaves behind a clean dataset, a fair way to evaluate strategies and an honest
record of what works and what does not, the next student who tries to beat the Vietnamese
market can start where this work ends rather than where it began.

I am grateful to my supervisor, Assoc. Prof. Ha Viet Uyen Synh, for pointing me towards this
topic and for giving me the freedom to shape it, and to the School of Computer Science and
Engineering for the knowledge that made the work possible. Finally, I thank my family and
friends for their patience and support throughout.
