\section{The Vietnamese Stock Market and How It Differs}
\label{sec:vn-market}

Much of the published work on machine learning for stock trading uses data from the United
States, and much of the work on Asian markets uses mainland China. \gls{hose} shares some features with China but few with
the United States, and several of its differences change what a model can learn and what a
strategy can earn. Table~\ref{tab:market-compare} summarises them; the rules used in the
backtest are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:vn-rules}.

These differences have direct consequences for the models in this thesis.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Returns are truncated, risk is not.} No stock can move more than 7\% in a day, so
    almost no daily return lies beyond three standard deviations (Section~\ref{sec:data-eda}).
    A large fall is instead spread over several sessions locked at the floor, during which a
    sell order may not fill. Price limits delay price discovery rather than remove the move
    \citep{kim1997price}. A model must therefore treat a predicted exit as uncertain and a
    stop-loss as a wish, not a guarantee.
  \item \textbf{Every position is held for days.} A share bought today cannot be sold until
    settlement, so same-day reversals and intraday strategies are impossible, and a wrong signal
    is paid for over several sessions.
  \item \textbf{Only rises can be traded.} Without short selling, a model can profit only from
    stocks that go up; in the 2022 bear market ($-40\%$) the only defensive action was to hold
    cash. Long--short anomalies that drive much of the US literature cannot be exploited here.
  \item \textbf{Few independent bets.} The liquid cross-section is about 150 stocks, and the most
    traded ones move together (mean pairwise correlation 0.39). Ranking-based strategies have
    far fewer independent choices than on a market of thousands of stocks, and five positions
    may behave like two.
  \item \textbf{Short, imperfect history.} A clean, survivorship-free daily history covers under
    seven years and three market regimes. Every configuration tried is a chance to fit noise, so
    corrections for multiple testing are essential rather than optional
    \citep{bailey2014dsr,bailey2017pbo}.
  \item \textbf{Data must be repaired before use.} Unlike the curated databases behind most US
    studies, the available feeds contain placeholder prices, incomplete corporate-action
    adjustment and silent paging errors (Section~\ref{sec:data-quality}).
  \item \textbf{Costs bite harder at small size.} A minimum order of 100 shares of a 100{,}000~VND
    stock is 10 million VND, so a small account cannot size positions finely, and fees on both
    sides plus the sales tax are paid on every round trip.
  \item \textbf{Speculative operators.} Thinly traded stocks can be moved by coordinated trading
    that paints chart patterns \citep{allen1992manipulation,aggarwal2006manipulation}. No public
    record marks which episodes were manipulated, so the system flags the risk and restricts
    itself to the liquid universe rather than claiming to detect it.
\end{enumerate}

Models and results published on US data cannot therefore be carried over as they are. The
closest analogue is the Chinese A-share market, which also has daily price limits, a 100-share
minimum, delayed selling and restricted short selling, but \gls{hose} has a tighter band, a
longer settlement delay and a much smaller liquid cross-section.
